# Legal, policy and platform risk

_Research dimension: Legal, policy and platform risks (Google ToS and SERP scraping legality, case law, AI Overview DOM fragility, copyright of stored extracts and AI Overview text, trademark, GDPR/CCPA, robots.txt etiquette, EU AI Act and LLM provider terms, billing/abuse)_

Gathered 2026-10-06. See [README.md](README.md) for method and caveats.

## Summary

As of 2026-10-06 the single biggest legal fact for this product is that Google is actively litigating against the exact data supply chain it depends on: Google LLC v. SerpApi (N.D. Cal., filed Dec 19 2025) was dismissed on July 20 2026 insofar as it concerned search results that contain no copyrighted content, but Google re-filed an amended DMCA §1201 complaint on Aug 10 2026 built on its content-licensing deals (including Reddit), SerpApi moved to dismiss again on Aug 24 2026, and no ruling on that second motion was findable yet. In parallel, Reddit v. SerpApi/Perplexity/Oxylabs (S.D.N.Y.) survived dismissal on July 31 2026 with the court holding that Google's SearchGuard bot-challenge is a DMCA "access control" even though humans can see the same content, and it kept Perplexity, a downstream consumer of scraped SERP data, in the case; that is the clearest signal that buyers of scraped Google data are not automatically insulated. Google's current Terms (effective July 30 2026) prohibit "using automated means to access content ... in violation of the machine-readable instructions on our web pages (for example, robots.txt ...)" and google.com/robots.txt disallows /search, and Google's spam policy names "scraping results for rank-checking purposes" as a violation, so every daily AI Overview capture is a ToS breach by whoever touches google.com; the product must therefore never hit Google directly, never use customer Google accounts, and route all capture through a SERP vendor behind an abstraction layer. The classic case law (Van Buren 2021, hiQ 2022, Meta v. Bright Data Jan 2024, X Corp v. Bright Data May 2024) makes CFAA and ToS claims weak against logged-off scrapers of public data, but hiQ still ended with a $500k judgment and permanent injunction on contract grounds, and the live threat has shifted to DMCA §1201 ($200–$2,500 statutory damages per act), which is exactly why vendor "legal shields" (SerpApi $2M, SearchApi $2M, both Production-tier-only, US-courts-only, and both excluding downstream use) matter but do not cover the SaaS's own use of the data. Google offers no licensed alternative: its only official search API (Custom Search JSON) is "closed to new customers" with existing users cut off by Jan 1 2027, and Google states it "does not offer a public search API for this type of data." Platform fragility is real and recurring: Google's Jan 2025 JavaScript/SearchGuard requirement broke rank trackers, num=100 was removed in Sept 2025, AI Overviews now often load asynchronously (SerpApi's page_token "expires within 1 minute"; DataForSEO doubles the price to $0.0012 for async AIO), the AIO layout changed in Oct 2024, Jan 2026, Feb 2026 and at I/O May 2026, and SerpApi's status page shows Google Search API degradation running Oct 1–6 2026, so the capture layer must store raw payloads, distinguish "no AIO" from "fetch failed", and be multi-vendor. Copyright risk from storing and analysing cited pages is manageable if the product behaves like a search-and-analysis tool (Authors Guild v. Google: full-text copying for search plus limited snippets is fair use) and not like a substitute (Thomson Reuters v. Ross, affirmed by the Third Circuit in Sept 2026: copying to build a competing product is not fair use); in the EU the Art. 4 DSM text-and-data-mining exception applies to commercial use unless the site opts out in machine-readable form (Hamburg OLG, Dec 10 2025), so honoring robots.txt and Cloudflare Content-Signals (ai-input/ai-train) is both etiquette and a legal safe harbor. Showing Google's AI Overview text to users is low risk in the US (purely machine-generated text is uncopyrightable per Thaler, cert denied Mar 2 2026, and the SerpApi court held non-copyrighted SERP content is not DMCA-protected) provided you do not store Knowledge-Panel images or licensed media and do not train models on AIO output, which Google's ToS expressly forbids. Naming must avoid "Google" or "AI Overviews" in the product/company/domain name; Google's brand rules permit only "works with / compatible with Google Search™" phrasing with a trademark legal line, and "AI Overviews" is not on Google's published trademark list but "SGE™" and "Google Search™" are. Privacy obligations are those of an ordinary B2B processor (GDPR Art. 28 DPA, sub-processor list naming the SERP and LLM vendors, SCCs as DPF backup while the Latombe appeal is pending at the CJEU; CCPA applies only above $26.625M revenue or 100k consumers but has no B2B exemption). LLM-side, Anthropic's commercial terms (no training, customer owns outputs, IP indemnity for paid use, 30-day deletion or per-org zero-data-retention) and OpenAI's API terms (no training since Mar 2023, 30-day abuse logs, ZDR on /v1/responses and /v1/chat/completions) are suitable, but both require the customer to have rights to its Inputs and Anthropic's AUP bars "circumvent[ing] the guardrails or terms of other platforms", so keep the LLM step to analysis of excerpts rather than wholesale re-publication. The EU AI Act Art. 50 transparency duties apply from Aug 2 2026 (machine-readable marking of generated text, deployer disclosure of published AI text unless substantively human-edited), which the content-brief generator should satisfy with an embedded marker and an "AI-assisted, human review required" workflow. Finally, Judge Mehta dismissed the Chegg/Penske antitrust suits against AI Overviews on Sept 30 2026, so AI Overviews are not going away in the US, which is good for product demand but also means Google's incentive to harden SearchGuard and sue scrapers persists.
