# Design panel: three proposals, three judges

Run on 2026-10-06 after the research. Three architects wrote independent proposals against the same verified research and constraints; three judges (a skeptical CTO, an agency owner who would be the customer, a growth PM) scored each on feasibility for a solo founder with Claude Code in about eight weeks, fidelity to the six steps, cost to serve, differentiation and risk (10 = lowest risk). `PLAN.md` adopts the winner with the grafts listed in `docs/decisions/0001-plan-adopted.md`. The full synthesis the panel produced is kept as `docs/research/appendix-design-synthesis.md` for traceability; where it differs from `PLAN.md`, the plan is authoritative.

## Proposals

### MVP-first: the smallest product that proves agencies and SEO freelancers will pay monthly for Jake Ward's six-step loop on ONE query, shippable in 6 weeks (2 weeks pilot kernel + 4 weeks SaaS) by one person with Claude Code, built almost entirely from managed services, with nothing in the pilot thrown away.

Legiit Overviews (name pending counsel): pick one buying-intent query; we capture its AI Overview in Google Search every day for 7 days, count what keeps showing up, reverse-engineer the pages it keeps citing, hand you a brief for something better, and tell you the day your page gets cited.

Why its author thought it would win: No competitor checked in the research does Jake Ward's loop end to end: 30+ tools sell breadth across engines and brand share-of-voice, Mentions.so (the thread's own tool) monetizes step 2 only, and reviewers' top complaint is 'tell me what to do next'. This proposal sells exactly steps 3-6 for one query at a time, prices the brief instead of the scrape, and uses Legiit's existing GEO demand (six of fifteen September 2026 best-sellers were AIO/GEO-themed) as both distribution and fulfilment. It is the smallest build that still honours every constraint: the Milestone 0 CLI is the product kernel rather than a throwaway, so two weeks of pilot work become the SaaS's providers, normalizer, metrics, prompts and fetcher; the global capture pool plus hash and near-duplicate dedup keep Starter COGS near $0.50 and Pro near $1.20 per query-month against $3.90-3.96 of revenue; postback-first scheduling keeps Inngest inside the free tier to ~50 customers; and every expensive or legally exposed surface (public pages, API/MCP, AI Mode, city targeting, Search Console, draft scoring, Agency tier) is deferred until paying customers exist. The willingness-to-pay question gets answered three times before month two: manual playbooks in the pilot, the beta cohort converting in week 5, and five subscriptions within 14 days of launch.

### analysis-engine-first: optimise the tracking, pattern-analysis, citation reverse-engineering, brief and attribution engine for fidelity, measurable accuracy and compounding cross-customer learning; the UI is a plain renderer of derived rows.

A TypeScript engine that turns every render of one Google AI Overview into evidence-linked claims, entities, citations and page structures, counts what keeps showing up with honest sample sizes, reverse-engineers the winning pages, writes a brief whose every line points at evidence, tracks whether the published page gets cited, and gets sharper with every query any customer tracks.

Why its author thought it would win: Every incumbent sells breadth across engines and a presence score; none verified in the research maps citations to individual claims, counts recurrence across renders with sample sizes, reverse-engineers the cited pages into structural features, or writes a brief whose items point at evidence. Those are exactly steps 3-5 of the thread and exactly what reviewers say is missing ('what do I do next'). This proposal makes that engine the product: deterministic normalisation with a status and visibility model so counts are never contaminated by truncation or outages; LLMs used only for extraction, adjudication and synthesis, never for counting; every metric defined once with an explicit denominator; evals that fail CI when quality slips; and a global capture pool so the second customer on a query pays nothing for history and the twentieth series in a vertical starts producing benchmarks nobody else has. Because only derived data ever leaves the system and third-party pages are reduced to features, embeddings and short quotes, the engine is also the safest legal posture available while Google litigates the scrape layer. The result compounds: more series produce better priors, better priors produce better briefs and scorers, and post-publish attribution turns each customer's outcome into labelled data for the next one.

### Distribution-first via Legiit: agencies and Legiit GEO/on-page sellers are the primary users, every analysis ends as a fulfilable work order (self, agency team, or a vetted Legiit seller scored against the brief), white-label client reports are a core tier feature, and the free "track ONE AI Overview" hook exists to feed the marketplace. Legiit SSO, order creation and seller tooling are a later phase that the data model is shaped not to block.

Legiit Overviews (name pending counsel) tracks one Google AI Overview for 7+ days, turns the evidence into a brief-shaped work order, and routes that order to an agency team or a Legiit GEO seller who is scored against the brief before the page ships and tracked after it goes live.

Why its author thought it would win: Every incumbent sells breadth (many engines, many prompts, a visibility score) and leaves the buyer with the complaint reviewers repeat: 'what do I do next'. This product sells depth on one query and ends every analysis with an order. Legiit supplies what no competitor has: 25,000+ freelancers, tens of thousands of buyers at a ~$90 average order, a live GEO category whose gigs already promise AI Overview outcomes, and adjacent SaaS (Command Center $19/mo, Leads $47-497/mo) whose subscribers are the first customers. Agencies are the proven paying segment in this category (Mentions.so $399 white-label, Peec agency bundles, SE Ranking Agency Pack), and they get client workspaces, pitch mode and branded reports in the top tier from day one. The global capture pool makes popular queries nearly free to add and gives pitch queries instant history; the Brief Pack and seller claim link turn every seller into an acquisition channel because sellers bring their own buyers; the draft score gives agencies a QA gate for freelancers and gives Legiit buyers a reason to trust a $149-299 build. Milestone 0 sells the deliverable through Legiit before any UI exists, so the SaaS is built only on evidence of willingness to pay. Pricing sits above the $1.2-2.0 per-prompt norm because the unit is a brief and a build, not a scrape, and the legal posture (never touching google.com, derived-only outward surfaces, data minimisation on cited pages) keeps it on the analysis-tool side of the line while the category's data supply is in court.

## Judges' verdicts

### Judge 1: winner MVP-first

Scored as the agency owner who would pay for this, against the shared non-negotiables (Milestone 0 gate, global capture pool, tiered cadence, provider abstraction, derived-only outward surfaces). All three proposals share the same supply-chain legal exposure: Reddit v. SerpApi (corrected finding) treated Perplexity as a direct circumventor because it set the query parameters and ran the queries itself through SerpApi, which is exactly how every proposal captures AIOs, so vendor Legal Shields do not cover the SaaS's own use; that lowers every risk score equally and does not change the ordering. MVP-first wins on the only axis that matters for a solo founder in eight weeks: it is the only plan that ships all six steps, takes money, and keeps the surface area small enough that nothing needs counsel before launch, while leaving the Agency features and marketplace loop additive. Analysis-engine-first builds the product I would most like to use in month six but has no buyable product until week 10-12 and prices Starter at 5 queries ($7.80/query, 4x the market). Distribution-first describes the tier I would actually buy (client workspaces, pitch mode, white-label, draft score as a freelancer acceptance gate) but its defining Phases 5-6 land after week 9, its Pro tier is 0-65% margin by its own numbers, and the fulfilment network it depends on does not exist yet: a live check of Legiit GEO subcategory 158 today shows only $6-$75 LinkedIn guest-post and brand-mention gigs, zero page-build-from-brief gigs, and the Legiit MCP is a read-only catalog (search/list tools only), so Phase 6's order API is speculative. Recommendation: build MVP-first, but graft in three cheap ideas from the losers before launch: the $99 one-off Playbook SKU and slot accounting from Distribution-first, and evidence_refs plus the deterministic half of the draft scorer from Analysis-engine-first; and give Pro 2-3 seats, because one-user-per-org is the single thing most likely to make an agency choose Mentions.so Agency instead. Also re-pin Next.js to 16.3.x rather than the same-day 16.4.0 and verify the 3-executions-per-capture Inngest claim on the dev-server trace before relying on the Free tier.

**MVP-first.** The only plan that ships the full loop and takes money inside eight weeks, but six weeks is optimistic: Phase 0 packs ten tasks (two provider adapters, normalizer plus fixture corpus, metrics, clustering with a 200-pair eval at F1 >= 0.85, SSRF-guarded fetcher, playbook) into a calendar-gated fortnight, and Phase 1 puts the full schema, RLS, DST-tested scheduler, postback/sweeper/failover, canary and cost ledger into one week; expect 8-9 weeks. It pins next 16.4.0, released today, after the research said to avoid same-day releases. The '3 Inngest executions per capture' figure (Free covers ~500 Starter query-months) is asserted, not measured; the critique's estimate was 8-10, so the Vercel Workflows fallback may be needed much earlier. As the buyer: one user per org and no client workspaces means I cannot give an account manager a login or show a client anything without screenshots, which pushes agencies to Mentions.so Agency ($399, white-label, pitch workspace) for the reporting layer. Ten countries, no city targeting, and one timezone per country excludes the local-SEO agencies that are Legiit's core audience (SE Ranking: zero cross-city overlap on 6.3% of queries, concentrated in legal, health, real estate). Starter's 1 render/day against a 70% change probability makes the daily 'what changed' email noisy, and the first aha moment (the brief) still lands on day 7. Cutting the draft scorer leaves me with no acceptance gate for the writer I hand the brief to; the deterministic feature comparison against winners' medians is a reasonable substitute but is only offered at publish time, not on drafts. Markdown-only export is fine for v1 but writers and clients want PDF or Docs. Unit economics are the most honest of the three (Starter ~$0.50 vs $3.90, Pro ~$1.20 vs $3.96) and include the SerpApi-primary sensitivity, but the $0.008 Haiku-class batch extraction rests on an assumed 3K-in/2.5K-out and a 45% unique-render rate on Pro, both unmeasured until T0.10; Haiku's successor may also use the 4.7+ tokenizer (+30%). Phase 3's exit gate of three paying subscriptions from a ten-person beta is thin. Strengths that matter to a customer: 'no AIO yet, watching' with sibling suggestions, sample-size labels on every percentage, survival buckets, section-level citation mapping, first-seen/lost/regained emails, and a free tier whose abandoned trial costs about a cent.

Ideas to keep:
- packages/core as the pilot kernel imported unchanged by the SaaS, so Milestone 0 is not throwaway
- Postback-first scheduling (hourly planner, postback route, 2-step normalize, hourly sweeper) designed to hold Inngest near 3 executions per capture, with Vercel Workflows as the named fallback
- The daily 'what changed' diff email as the days 1-6 retention mechanism, with 'preliminary patterns' unlocking at day 3 under a low-confidence badge
- Deterministic feature comparison of the user's published URL against the winners' medians instead of an LLM rubric call
- Free-tier gating that makes an abandoned trial cost ~$0.01: no LLM before day 3, 3 cited pages, brief behind Starter, one series per verified email

**analysis-engine-first.** Ten weeks before a self-serve screen exists; at week 8 the founder has an engine, evals and a manual playbook channel, but nothing I can buy. The scope is the largest engineering bet of the three: Intl.Segmenter sentence refs validated against the schema, claim-to-passage matching with the WashU five-label scheme, a draft scorer, vertical_stats and domain_profile rollups with a priors API, feedback labels flowing into a CI eval with spend caps, a REST API and an MCP server, a model-regime detector, and a three-deep fetcher chain ending in self-hosted Playwright on Railway, which adds a worker host the lean plans avoid. CI gates at F1 >= 0.90 clustering, 97% entailment and 85% top-1 passage matching are stricter than MVP-first's 0.85 and can stall shipping when Haiku 4.5's successor forces recalibration. Pricing is the weakest point for an agency: Starter at 5 queries for $39 is $7.80 per query, four times the $1.2-2.0 norm and a tier cliff of the kind reviewers punish; Pro's 20 queries is two per client for a ten-client shop; and 'plain tables, no dashboards, no white-label, no client workspaces' means I cannot present any of it to a client. Margins are thinner than MVP-first (Pro ~59%, Agency ~50% by its own numbers) because the engine does more LLM work per query (page analyses, passage labelling, synthesis, weekly re-runs, draft scores), and at ~6 Inngest executions per capture Free covers about 46 Pro queries, so the $99 Inngest Pro bill arrives with the first handful of customers. The 'sampling honesty' argument leans on the Schulte preprint, which the skeptic noted excluded Google AI Overviews entirely. Vertical benchmarks publish only at k >= 20 series per (vertical, hl), so early customers never see the compounding layer that justifies the build order. Exposing API and MCP in Phase 4 before counsel has cleared any outward surface, plus the self-hosted Playwright path, raises the risk profile. What I would pay for is real: evidence_refs on every brief line, click-through from every count to the exact render and sentence, 'unsupported claims' as new-to-cite opportunities, renders_observable denominators that exclude collapsed renders, and loss-alert suppression during a model-regime window.

Ideas to keep:
- evidence_refs on every brief item and click-through from every count to the exact render and sentence
- Claim-to-passage matching that yields 'AIO claims no cited page supports' as explicit new-to-cite opportunities
- renders_observable denominators so expanded-only clusters are not diluted by present_collapsed renders
- Draft scorer: deterministic topic/entity/format/time-to-answer sub-scores against CORE-page medians before any LLM rubric
- Legal guards: page_passage with no text column, the 5x200-char quote cap enforced in code, and the 12-word-span check against stored competitor quotes
- Suppress loss alerts inside a model_regime window and show a platform-event banner instead

**Distribution-first via Legiit.** This is the tier I would actually buy: client workspaces, pitch mode that inherits pool history, token-protected branded reports, and a draft score as the acceptance gate for the freelancer I pay. But the plan's defining features sit in Phases 5-6 (weeks 9-12+), after an already full Phases 0-4 that end at week 9; in eight weeks the founder is mid-Phase 4 with no work orders, no Brief Packs, no seller accounts, no white-label. Phase 5 alone (seller org kind with scoped permissions, Playwright-printed PDFs on Vercel, branding, slot accounting, section matcher) is two or more solo weeks. The fulfilment premise is unproven today: a live check of Legiit GEO subcategory 158 returns only $6-$75 LinkedIn guest posts, brand mentions and backlinks and one $400 GBP gig, zero 'build a page from a brief' gigs, so the seller network must be recruited from scratch and the product's reputation rides on $10 sellers passing a draft score; the proposal's own risk list admits this. The Legiit MCP is a read-only catalog (search/list tools only; verified today and in the research), so Phase 6's order API and account linking are speculative and the attach-rate KPI depends on paste-the-order-URL reconciliation. Economics: Pro is 0-65% margin by its own numbers and is explicitly 'gated' to 20 queries or 2 renders/day pending measurement, which means pricing is not settled; Agency at $2.49 per slot carries 10 seats, unlimited clients, PDF rendering and free seller orgs that can trigger Sonnet rubric calls (capped at 10 per order per day), the broadest cost and abuse surface of the three; abandoned free trials cost $0.15-0.30 versus a cent in MVP-first. Legal surface is the widest: Brief Packs handed to third parties, white-label client reports, an embeddable widget, a 'verdict feed' for Legiit Leads, API/MCP, and a GSC flag; derived-only contract tests are the right mitigation, but none of it has been cleared by counsel and every one of these is redistribution-shaped. It also competes with Legiit Leads' existing 'AI Overview verdicts', which the proposal flags but does not resolve. Sensible calls: Next 16.3.x pin, city-level on Pro/Agency, organization.kind, work_order.external_ref so marketplace integration is additive, slot accounting where an extra render or device consumes a slot, and the $99 Playbook SKU sold both in-app and as a gig.

Ideas to keep:
- $99 one-off Playbook SKU (one query, 30 days at Pro cadence, report, brief, 3 draft scores) sold in-app via Stripe and as a Legiit gig, continuing the Milestone 0 deliverable
- Agency tier shape: client workspaces, pitch mode that inherits capture-pool history and converts without data loss, token-protected noindex branded report pages
- Slot accounting where each extra render/day or device on a query consumes one plan slot, with the cost shown before saving
- Draft score as the delivery acceptance gate with per-order score history, so agencies have an objective QA bar for freelancers
- organization.kind and work_order.external_ref/external_identity so marketplace linking is additive and never touches the capture pool
- Derived-only regex contract tests on every outward surface (packs, reports, widgets, feeds, API) asserting no snapshot block text or full reference lists

### Judge 2: winner MVP-first

Winner: MVP-first (37 vs 31 and 31). It is the only proposal that fits the solo-founder, ~8-week envelope with all six steps intact and a cost model that agrees with the committed scripts/unit_economics.py ($0.0077 per unique render on Haiku batch; Starter ~$0.50 vs $3.90, Pro ~$1.20-1.30 vs $3.96). Its 6-week calendar is optimistic by two to three weeks, but its scope is right: everything it cuts (agency tier, API/MCP, share pages, draft-score LLM, AI Mode, GSC, city targeting, Playwright, worker host) is either expensive, legally exposed, or unneeded before the first five subscriptions. Its postback-first Inngest topology (<= 3 executions per capture) is the only one of the three that keeps the Free tier to ~50 customers; the other two honestly budget 6 executions and hit Inngest Pro at roughly three Pro customers. Its legal surface is minimal: nothing leaves the tenant boundary, no public page, no API.

The analysis-engine proposal is the best engineering document and the right v1.1 engine roadmap, but it is a 12-week plan by its own admission, gated on ~500 hand-labelled eval items, ships API/MCP before billing, and prices Starter at $7.80/query. The distribution-first proposal has the smartest commercial idea (the $99 Playbook SKU) and the right framework pins, but it is 11+ weeks of scope, its Pro margin is unsettled because its own extraction figure contradicts its verified inputs, it never writes the cross-org RLS policy that seller access requires, it needs headless Chromium for PDFs that the stack does not provide, and Brief Packs, white-label PDFs and widgets are the widest redistribution surface while Google v. SerpApi is live.

Cross-cutting defects the winner must fix before the plan is written: (1) all three promise a day-7 report while restating the mandated gate of >= 5 capture-days AND >= 10 renders; at Starter/Free cadence (1 render/day) the full report cannot exist before day 10. Give Free and Starter a second daily render for the first 7 days (SERP cost only, ~$0.008 per query) or rename the day-7 deliverable 'preliminary'. (2) MVP-first pins next 16.4.0, released today; the research says avoid same-day releases; pin 16.3.x as the distribution proposal does, and drop the unverified patch pins. (3) MVP-first's undici-only fetcher needs a blocked-page rate measured in the pilot (T0.9) with Firecrawl promoted to primary if it exceeds ~20%. (4) Borrow from the losers for v1.1 in this order: the Playbook one-off SKU and work-order-with-external_ref paste (cheap, revenue-positive), renders_observable denominators and n-based confidence labels (cheap, honesty), the derived-only API/MCP contract tests and the claim-to-passage 'unsupported claims' module (engine depth), and organization.kind plus external_identity reserved in the schema now so none of this needs a tenancy migration later.

**MVP-first.** The '6 weeks' is marketing. Phase 1 alone (full schema + RLS + DST-safe slot planner + postback route + sweeper + SerpApi failover with idempotency + provider health + canary + cost ledger + batch extraction + clustering) is two weeks of integration work, not one; Phase 2 stacks auth, Turnstile, the add-query flow, dashboard, patterns page and five email templates into another single week. Realistic total is 8-9 weeks, which still fits the brief, so the plan is honest in scope and dishonest only in calendar. Two concrete errors: (1) it pins next 16.4.0, released today, when the research explicitly says to avoid same-day framework releases (Proposal 3 correctly pins 16.3.x); (2) several pinned patch versions (better-auth 1.7.7, inngest 4.21.1, defuddle 0.19.4, voyageai 0.4.0, stripe 23.0.0, resend 6.32.1) are not in the verified research and read as invented, and 'Firecrawl Free 1,000 credits/mo' is unverified (the committed unit-economics doc budgets Firecrawl Hobby at $16). The cited-page fetcher is plain undici with no JS rendering and Firecrawl only env-flagged; with Cloudflare's Sept 2026 defaults the proposal's own risk list admits >30% blocked pages would gut the coverage matrix, and nothing in the acceptance criteria measures the blocked rate during the pilot. The 'Free: pattern report at day 7' promise collides with the mandated gate (>= 5 capture-days AND >= 10 renders): at 1 render/day the full report cannot exist before day 10, so the free hook and Starter will only ever show the low-confidence preliminary view on day 7 unless the first week gets a second render (SERP cost only, ~$0.008/query). The 'missed postback' path is an hourly sweeper, so a failed DataForSEO task is noticed up to ~80 minutes late rather than via step.waitForEvent; acceptable, but the stated '<= 3 executions per capture' depends on cluster-and-metrics not running per snapshot, which T1.6 leaves ambiguous. Cost model is the most credible of the three and matches the committed script ($0.0077 per unique render on Haiku batch; Starter ~$0.50 vs $3.90 revenue; Pro ~$1.20-1.30 vs $3.96), but $3.90 per query is 2x the Mentions.so/Otterly norm and the plan has no evidence a Starter buyer accepts 10 queries at $39 beyond the research's say-so. Cutting API/MCP and the draft scorer removes two of the review-visible differentiators competitors already advertise; fine for v1 but it must be a stated v1.1 commitment, not a cut.

Ideas to keep:
- Postback-first Inngest topology (hourly planner submits 100 tasks in one step, postback route emits one event, 2-step normalize) with '<= 3 executions per successful capture' asserted on a dev-server trace, keeping Inngest Free to ~50 paying customers
- Daily 'what changed' email rendered only when a diff exists, with a Resend 100/day guard that queues and logs a 'move to Pro' warning; this is the only thing that fills days 1-6 of a trial
- Deterministic publish flow: run the page-feature extractor on the user's URL and show it beside the winners' medians, replacing the LLM draft scorer at zero marginal cost
- packages/core with no framework imports shared by apps/cli and apps/web, so the Milestone 0 pilot is the product kernel; T0.5's empirical JSON-vs-HTML 'Show more' check recorded in docs/fixtures.md
- Structured-output constraint tests on every zod schema (no recursion, additionalProperties false, minItems <= 1, <= 24 optional fields) plus the stop_reason guard before trusting parsed_output
- Phase 3 exit criteria tied to measured cost_rollup (<= $0.70 Starter / <= $1.40 Pro per query-month) rather than to a spreadsheet

**analysis-engine-first.** By its own schedule this is a 12-week plan (Phase 5 'first SaaS sprint' is weeks 10-12) and it says so: 'Engine-first sequencing means 10 weeks before a self-serve UI.' That fails the 8-week bar by 50% before slippage, and the slippage will be large because the plan is gated on hand-labelled evals a solo founder must produce: 200 claim pairs, 50 snapshots with verified claim lists, 100 claim-to-passage pairs, 100 entity mentions, 50 cited pages with hand-labelled heading/table/schema counts, plus a thresholds.ts 'generated, not hand-edited' and a Sonnet-judge rubric. That is roughly 500 human labels and weeks of calibration before the first paying org exists, and every threshold must be redone when the Haiku-class alias changes (retirement 'not sooner than Oct 15', nine days out). The architecture quietly exceeds the constraint stack: 'self-hosted Playwright last' needs a worker host that is never named or priced, and the attribution layer embeds passages of every CORE cited page plus the user's page, which is fine legally (no text column) but adds a second embedding pipeline before anyone pays. Cost model is honest but weaker: the depth-20 anchor render, Sonnet synthesis per report, three weekly post-publish synthesis+matrix re-runs ($0.27), two sync draft scores ($0.12) and a regen ($0.14) push Starter COGS to ~$1.15 and Pro to ~$2.05; Pro margin is 59% before pool sharing and Agency 50%, and Inngest Pro ($99) arrives at ~46 Pro queries, i.e. about three Pro customers, because '<= 5 steps per capture' means 6 executions. Starter at 5 queries for $39 ($7.80/query, 4-6x the market's $1.2-2.0 norm) will convert poorly regardless of margin. The compounding layer (vertical_stats/domain_profile at k >= 20 series per vertical x hl) is cold-started for months and is invisible in a demo because the UI is deliberately 'plain tables'; the differentiation is real but unseen by the buyer. Shipping a public API and MCP server in Phase 4, before billing exists, is backwards: API keys 'on paid plans only' with no paid plans. Same day-7 vs >= 10-render collision as the others for Starter at 1 render/day. Strongest engineering document of the three; wrong order and wrong length for a solo founder who needs revenue in two months.

Ideas to keep:
- renders_observable denominators (exclude present_collapsed renders for expanded-only clusters) and confidence labels by n (< 10 low, 10-20 medium, >= 21 high) attached to every number and every email
- Claim-cluster-to-passage matching with the WashU five labels and the resulting 'unsupported claims' list as explicit new-to-cite opportunities, plus the code check that no 12-word span of the brief matches a stored competitor quote
- Eval harness in CI: thresholds.ts generated by the eval runner with the run id committed, a grep test that fails on any literal 'claude-' model id outside models.ts, and a spend-capped scheduled eval workflow
- Normalizer preserves unknown block types as type 'unknown' with raw text instead of dropping them, and JSON-schema validation failures feed provider_health so a Google layout change surfaces as a provider score drop
- Suppress loss alerts inside a model_regime window and show the platform-event banner instead; brief v2 at days 14 and 28 with a diff
- Derived-only contract tests for any API/MCP endpoint (no aio_block text, no full reference lists) and k >= 20 publication threshold for vertical/domain priors; both belong in v1.1, written exactly this way

**Distribution-first via Legiit.** Eleven weeks to the fulfilment loop (Phase 5 is weeks 9-11) and the self-serve app does not exist until week 9, so no subscription revenue for two months; Phase 6 is open-ended against a Legiit order API the research confirms does not exist (Legiit's MCP is a read-only catalog v1). Scope is the widest of the three: on top of the full loop it adds a work-order state machine, Brief Packs with expiring tokens, a third organization kind (seller) with order-scoped cross-org access, seller draft submission, draft scoring, slot accounting, client workspaces, pitch mode, branded report pages, Playwright-printed PDFs, an embeddable widget, a verdict feed, four Stripe SKUs including a one-off, and a Railway Playwright worker. Two technical holes: Playwright-printed PDFs need headless Chromium, which neither Vercel functions nor the 'only if fetching fails' Railway worker provide for, and the seller org's read access to another org's work_order and brief cannot be expressed by the org_id = current_setting RLS policy in the data model; it needs an explicit grant (brief_pack.claimed_by_org_id could serve) and the proposal never writes that policy, which is exactly where a client-data leak happens. The unit economics are internally inconsistent: the 'verified inputs' line says extraction is $0.01-0.015 in batch, then the tier math uses $0.01-0.03, and the committed script says $0.0077; the result is a Pro tier that the proposal itself admits may run at '0-65%' margin, which is really ~45-65% once the arithmetic is fixed, but the fact that it shipped the pessimistic figure and a conditional 'gate Pro to 20 queries' shows the pricing is not settled. Seller-driven draft scores (10 per order per day, sync Sonnet $0.06 each) are a cost leak paid by the buyer's subscription; the 20%-coverage rubric guard helps but there is no per-seller daily LLM budget. Legal surface is the weakest: Brief Packs leave the tenant boundary, white-label PDFs and widgets are redistribution surfaces, and the proposal says so in its own risk list; derived-only regex tests are necessary but a regex is not a legal posture. Fulfilment quality risk is real and under-mitigated: Legiit GEO gigs today are $10-79 LinkedIn posts and schema setups, and a bad $149 build delivered under the product's brief damages the product, not the seller. Internal cannibalisation with Legiit Leads' existing 'AI Overview verdicts' is acknowledged but unresolved. It correctly pins Next 16.3.x and TypeScript 6.x, and the Playbook SKU is the smartest commercial idea in any of the three proposals. Same day-7 vs >= 10-render collision for Starter and the free hook.

Ideas to keep:
- Playbook $99 one-off SKU (one query, 30 days at Pro cadence, report, brief, 3 draft scores) sold in-app via Stripe and as a Legiit GEO gig; it bridges Milestone 0's manual sales into the product and monetises free users who will not subscribe
- Work order with fulfilment_channel enum, external_ref paste of a Legiit order URL, and deep links into GEO (158) / On-Site (17) with the pack token: zero-integration fulfilment that does not block on an order API that does not exist
- organization.kind enum (agency | brand | seller | internal) and a reserved external_identity table so Legiit SSO and client workspaces are additive to the schema without a migration of tenancy
- Draft score as a delivery gate with the cost guard (rubric call skipped when deterministic coverage < 20%) and deterministic sub-scores identical across runs
- Pitch mode: a prospect's query flagged is_pitch inherits pool history and converts to a client without losing analysis_run rows
- Pin Next 16.3.x and TypeScript 6.x on day 0 rather than the same-day 16.4 release, and batch one platform event into one email per org

### Judge 3: winner MVP-first

Scored against the verified research (digest.txt, research.json skeptic verdicts, docs/source-thread.md) from a retention-and-margins lens. Totals: MVP-first 38, analysis-engine-first 32, distribution-first 30. MVP-first wins because it is the only proposal that puts a paid, self-serve product in front of customers inside the 8-week budget (its 6-week claim is really 8-9 once the calendar-gated waits are counted, but the other two reach their first self-serve UI at weeks 9-12), has the cheapest cost to serve (~$0.50 Starter / ~$1.20 Pro against $3.90-3.96 revenue, break-even at 5 Starter subscriptions, $175-200 fixed), has the narrowest legal surface (no API, no share pages, no packs to third parties, no white-label), and builds the days 1-6 retention mechanism (daily what-changed email, preliminary patterns at day 3) into the first UI sprint. Its weaknesses are retention details rather than structure: no minimum-n or regime-window suppression on loss emails, per-query rather than per-org digests, one seat per org, an unmodelled SerpApi plan upgrade in the fallback scenario, an optimistic 3-executions-per-capture Inngest figure, and a same-day Next.js pin - all cheap to fix by importing specific ideas from the other two. Analysis-engine-first has the best fidelity and the deepest moat (evidence-linked claims, unsupported-claim opportunities, calibrated evals, honest denominators), but it is a 12-week engine with 500+ human labels, a 5-query Starter, Inngest Pro triggered by the third Pro customer, city-level series that defeat its own pool-sharing premise, and vertical priors that need k >= 20 series per vertical and so stay invisible for months; its retention and evidence ideas should be folded into the winner's v1.1. Distribution-first has a genuinely unique end state (brief -> scored seller draft -> tracked page) but the differentiating loop arrives in weeks 9-11 on unverified Legiit APIs, Legiit's GEO gigs today are $7-25 LinkedIn posts rather than page builds that could satisfy the thread's 'give Google something NEW' step, its Pro margin is 0-65% by its own numbers, agency support is an uncounted COGS, it cannibalises Legiit Leads and Command Center, and it opens the widest redistribution surface while counsel has not cleared share pages; its Playbook SKU, work_order/external_ref shape, slot accounting and pitch-mode inheritance are worth adopting as the winner's month-2 additions.

**MVP-first.** The 6-week calendar is fiction, but it is the only plan that lands a paid SaaS inside 8 weeks. Phase 1 (week 3) stuffs schema+RLS, slot scheduler, postback, sweeper, failover, health, canary, cost ledger and extraction jobs into five working days; Phase 2's exit needs 10 beta users to reach 5-of-7 capture-days (7+ calendar days) while Phase 3 is labelled week 5. Read it as 8-9 weeks with overlapped waits and it holds. Retention is where it is weakest: the brief (aha moment) lands on day 7, Starter's single daily render has a single-run SE of ~0.37, and detect-wins fires first_seen/lost/regained emails on every snapshot with no minimum-n and no regime-window suppression (Proposal 2 has both), so a Starter customer will get a 'lost' email after one absent render and churn on a sampling artefact. The per-query 'what changed' email at a 70% daily change rate is ~5 emails/week/query; a 10-query Starter gets 50/week, which is unsubscribe territory - it needs a per-org digest. Pricing is defensible ($3.90/query vs the $1.2-2.0 norm, justified by the brief) but one user per org at $99 Pro, against Mentions.so's unlimited seats on every tier, hands the proven agency segment to competitors for no COGS saving. Margins: base case ~$0.50 Starter / ~$1.20 Pro is credible, but the dedup assumption (45% unique at 3x2 renders) is unmeasured and intra-day variance is as large as day-to-day, so 70% unique is plausible (Pro ~$1.56, still fine). The real cliff is the fallback scenario: if DataForSEO declines ToS 7.1, Pro COGS ~$3.00 vs $3.96 revenue, and the SerpApi Production plan's 15,000 searches is consumed by ~1.7 Pro customers at two searches per capture (25 x 180 x 2 = 9,000), so the 'fallback' needs a SerpApi plan upgrade that is not modelled. The 3-executions-per-capture Inngest claim ignores the hourly sweeper, the batch assembler's step.sleep polling steps and cluster-and-metrics; expect 5-6, so Free runs out nearer 25-30 paying customers than 50. It pins next 16.4.0, released today, against the research's own advice. Cited-page fetching without JS rendering and Firecrawl only env-flagged means Cloudflare-defaulted publishers punch holes in the coverage matrix, degrading the brief that justifies the price. Finally, the Phase 3/4 exits are sales gates (3 then 5 subscriptions) on a seller-skewed Legiit audience, and without API/MCP, seats or white-label the agency buyer has little reason to switch.

Ideas to keep:
- packages/core as the shared kernel so the Milestone 0 CLI becomes the SaaS's providers, normalizer, metrics, prompts and fetcher unchanged
- Postback-first scheduling (hourly planner, postback route emits one event, 2-step normalize, hourly sweeper) to keep Inngest executions per capture minimal
- Daily 'what changed' email from the jsdiff diff engine so days 1-6 of the trial are not an empty dashboard (but make it a per-org digest)
- Pure-SQL 7-day report over extracted claims with the only post-extraction LLM calls being the coverage matrix and the brief; no synthesis narrative
- 'No AIO yet, watching' as a tracked state with zero LLM spend until an AIO appears, plus the disciplined cut list (no API, no share pages, no draft scoring, no agency tier in v1)
- Markdown-only brief export so the EU AI Act marker always travels with the artefact

**analysis-engine-first.** This is the best engine design and the worst business plan for a solo founder with an 8-week budget: the first self-serve UI is weeks 10-12, so inside 8 weeks it ships zero SaaS and the only revenue is the manual Legiit playbook gig. The eval harness is a second product: 200 labelled claim pairs, 50 snapshots with verified claim lists, 100 labelled entity mentions, 100 claim-to-passage pairs and 50 hand-labelled pages (500+ human labels) all produced by the one person who is also writing the code, with thresholds (F1 >= 0.90, >= 97% entailment, >= 85% top-1, >= 95% feature agreement) set before any data exists. Haiku 4.5's retirement ('not sooner than Oct 15') forces recalibration of four separately calibrated components (clustering, adjudication, passage matching, entity resolution), not one. Margins look fine on Starter (85%) only because Starter is 5 queries for $39 - the tightest cap in a category where Otterly's 15 and Semrush's 25 already draw complaints - and are thin where revenue concentrates: Pro 59% and Agency 50% by its own numbers, lower once brief v2 at days 14 and 28 (two extra Opus briefs per query in month one), the per-report Sonnet synthesis call, weekly re-runs and the +30% tokenizer are counted. Fixed-cost cliffs arrive absurdly early: at ~6 Inngest executions per capture, Free covers ~46 Pro queries, so the third Pro customer ($297 MRR) triggers Inngest Pro ($99), plus Firecrawl Standard ($83) and a self-hosted Playwright tier that the stack research told you not to run. City-level locations in v1 fragment the global pool - every city is a separate series with zero sharing - which quietly undermines the compounding-layer premise, and vertical priors need k >= 20 series per (vertical, hl): with 10 customers x 5 queries you reach that in one vertical after months, so the headline differentiator is invisible for the first half year. Building a public API, API-key management, rate limits and an MCP server in Phase 4 before any UI or paying customer exists is inverted priority. The 0.98 near-dup reuse hides exactly the change buyers of 'best X' queries care about (one swapped recommendation), which it admits. Retention mechanics are actually the strongest of the three (confidence labels by n, loss-alert suppression in regime windows, brief v2 with diff) but they arrive at week 8-10. Legal surface is wider than Proposal 1: passage embeddings of third-party pages stored forever, 5 quotes per page across customers, share pages, API and MCP at v1, and a planned learned model on outcome data.

Ideas to keep:
- evidence_refs on every brief item and claim-to-passage matching with WashU five labels, so 'AIO claims no cited page supports' become the new-to-cite opportunities
- renders_observable denominators that exclude present_collapsed renders for expanded-only clusters, and confidence labels by n (< 10 low, 10-20 medium, >= 21 high) on every number
- Loss-alert suppression inside a model_regime window with a platform-event banner instead, and win/loss emails that state n and confidence
- thresholds.ts generated by the eval runner and committed with the run id, never hand-edited; prompt_version and model alias stamped on every derived row
- Batch custom_id = jobType:id:schemaVersion with idempotent re-runs and an 'unknown' block type preserved with raw text rather than dropped
- Self-promotional detection: a recommended entity whose supporting reference is its own registrable domain, separating 'cited' from 'recommended'

**Distribution-first via Legiit.** The differentiator (work orders, Brief Packs, seller claim accounts, scored drafts, white-label client reports, pitch mode) lands in weeks 9-11, and the Legiit integration in week 12+ against APIs the research could not verify (Legiit's MCP is a read-only catalog v1). Cut to 8 weeks, this ships a three-tier tracker with a Playbook SKU and no fulfilment loop - a me-too product in the one category where every competitor already has an agency tier. Phase 5 is 7 tasks in 2 weeks and includes the single biggest solo-founder time sink available: a permission model with three actor kinds (agency/brand/seller), order-scoped seller accounts, a work-order state machine, expiring pack tokens and Playwright-printed PDFs (which need a browser host Vercel does not give you). The fulfilment premise is also at odds with the thread's step 5: the research shows Legiit GEO gigs today are $7-25 LinkedIn posts, brand mentions and schema setups, not page builds, and a $10 seller cannot supply the customer's original data or first-hand experience that the brief's new-to-cite section demands; the draft score as a delivery gate helps but cannot manufacture evidence, and low-quality deliveries damage Legiit's own reputation. Margins are the weakest of the three by its own arithmetic: Pro '0-65%' gross margin with a post-launch plan to cut Pro to 20 queries if the unique-content rate exceeds 40%; Agency $249 for 100 slots, unlimited clients, white-label, pitch mode and 10 seats means agency support is the hidden COGS, and agencies will immediately buy city and mobile variants (good for slots, bad for pool sharing). Seller draft scores at 10 per order per day with a Sonnet rubric guarded only by a 20% coverage skip can run ~$18 per order per month. The free hook is explicitly designed for volume to feed the marketplace, yet an abandoned signup costs $0.15-0.30 (10-20x Proposal 1), so growth spikes become vendor bills. Channel conflict is built in: the Playbook is sold in-app and as a Legiit gig with an unverified marketplace commission; Legiit Leads already sells 'AI Overview verdicts' on every tier; a 30-day free Starter for Command Center subscribers cannibalises Starter. It has the widest outward data surface of the three - Brief Packs to non-customers, token report pages, embeddable widgets, verdict feeds, seller proof-of-work reports, API/MCP - each derived-only by regex contract test but each another redistribution argument for Google or DataForSEO, with share pages not yet cleared by counsel. Retention is reasonable (daily diff email, 14/28-day re-runs, stable-baseline badge) but the agency's client-report cadence, its real retention hook, is Phase 5. Credit where due: it pins Next 16.3.x and TypeScript 6.x on day 0, the only proposal that follows the research's same-day-release warning.

Ideas to keep:
- Playbook $99 one-off SKU (one query, 30 days at Pro cadence, report, brief, 3 draft scores) as the mid-trial conversion step and the in-app version of the Milestone 0 deliverable
- work_order with fulfilment_channel and external_ref plus external_identity reserved, so fulfilment and Legiit linking are additive without a schema migration
- Slot accounting where each extra render/day or device consumes one plan slot - a clean primitive for pricing cadence instead of per-tier hard-coding
- organization.kind, client, and is_pitch queries that inherit pool history and convert to a client without losing analysis_run rows
- Draft score as a delivery gate with deterministic sub-scores identical across runs and the rubric call skipped below 20% coverage
- Pin Next 16.3.x / TypeScript 6.x / Drizzle 0.45.x on day 0 rather than a same-day release
